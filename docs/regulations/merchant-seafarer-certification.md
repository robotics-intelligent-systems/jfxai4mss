## Source translation: regulatory context

In commercial maritime operations, navigation and cargo-transport qualifications and certificates are governed by international standards established by the International Maritime Organization (IMO), through the STCW Convention (Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers). They are administered nationally by each country's maritime authority: in Peru, DICAPI (*Dirección General de Capitanías y Guardacostas*, Directorate General of Captaincies and Coastguards).

Merchant-marine seafarers are classified according to their level of responsibility, navigation area and the tonnage/power of the vessel they operate.

## Source translation: 1. Seafarer hierarchy and certification

### A. Deck department — navigation and cargo operations

The deck department is responsible for handling the vessel, manoeuvring, stability and cargo stowage.

| Role | Responsibilities described by the source |
| --- | --- |
| Merchant Marine Master | Highest authority on board; holds overall responsibility for the safety of the vessel, crew, cargo and navigation. |
| Chief Mate | Second in command; manages maintenance work and watch assignments and has direct responsibility for the cargo stowage, securing and discharge plan. |
| Second and Third Deck Officers / Watchkeeping Officers | Take bridge watches and attend to navigation equipment and lifesaving/safety systems. |
| Coastal / Navigation Skipper | Licensed to command smaller-tonnage vessels in cabotage services, coastal areas, bays or ports. |
| Deck Ratings / Seamen / Helmsmen | Operational personnel responsible for steering, lookout duties, mooring and maintenance. |

### B. Engine department — propulsion and maintenance

The engine department ensures the operation of the propulsion plant, generators and auxiliary systems.

| Role | Responsibilities described by the source |
| --- | --- |
| Chief Engineer | Responsible for all machinery, fuel consumption and the vessel's technical maintenance. |
| Second, Third and Fourth Engineer Officers | Supervise engine-room watches and mechanical and electrical maintenance. |
| Engine Operators / Maintenance Mechanics | Ratings providing operational support for the power plant. |

## Source translation: 2. Licence types by navigation area

| Navigation area | Source description |
| --- | --- |
| Ocean-going navigation / international maritime navigation | Permits command or service as an officer on vessels without distance-from-coast or geographical limitations. |
| Cabotage / national or regional navigation | Authorizes commercial navigation between ports of the same country or within defined coastal areas. |
| Inland waterway and lake navigation | Specialized qualifications for commercial transport on navigable rivers, such as those in the Amazon region, and lakes, where specific licences for skippers and river pilots apply. |

## Source translation: 3. Endorsements and qualifications for specialized cargo ships

The source states that a deck or engineering certificate alone is insufficient for merchant cargo-transport operations, and that STCW proficiency courses and special endorsements must be completed according to vessel type. Its cargo table is reproduced below; the editorial notes distinguish cargo-code obligations from individually issued certificates.

| Cargo vessel / cargo type | Certification or special endorsement stated by the source | Application and safety topics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Container ships / general cargo | Training in lashing, cargo securing and stability | Container handling, heavy-lift cargo and calculation of heeling moments. |
| Tankers — oil, chemical and gas / LNG carriers | Certificate of proficiency in tanker operations (“Dangerous Cargo Handling”) | Inert atmospheres, explosion prevention, pumping systems and management of dangerous goods in bulk. |
| Bulk carriers | IMSBC Code and cargo-liquefaction training | Stability control against grain/mineral cargo shifting and corrosion prevention. |
| Dangerous goods under the IMDG Code | Certification in handling packaged dangerous cargo | Identification, labelling, segregation and response to chemical emergencies on board. |

## Source translation: 4. General issuance and revalidation requirements

- **Seaman's Book:** official document recording identity, category, certificates and the history of days served at sea on board.
- **Maritime medical fitness:** physical, hearing and eyesight assessment under STCW standards and those of the local maritime authority.
- **Continued competence / revalidation:** certificates and endorsements are revalidated periodically, usually every 5 years, by demonstrating a minimum amount of sea service or completing refresher courses.

## Editorial verification notes

Review date: 7 October 2026. This is a limited check of official references, not a complete review of current Peruvian certification procedures or of an individual's eligibility.

| Issue | Editorial finding and reference |
| --- | --- |
| International framework | The [IMO STCW overview](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/humanelement/pages/stcw-convention.aspx) describes the international training, certification and watchkeeping framework. The source's role names and grouped duties are descriptive, not a verified mapping to every national certificate title or STCW competence level. |
| Peruvian administration | An [official Peruvian government description of DICAPI](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/fondepes/noticias/946317-dicapi-cuales-son-sus-funciones-vinculadas-a-la-pesca) identifies its maritime-authority role and oversight of seafarers within its competence. The exact certificate, tonnage, propulsion-power and area restrictions must be established from the applicable DICAPI procedure and the issued credential. |
| Navigation areas | The source's three-area classification is a simplified overview. Its “without geographical limitations” wording does not by itself remove certificate restrictions, flag-state recognition requirements or other operating permissions. No equivalence between ocean-going, coastal and inland credentials was established here. |
| Cargo codes versus personal certification | The [IMO cargo overview](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/safety/pages/cargoes.aspx) lists separate instruments for dangerous goods, solid bulk cargo, grain and cargo securing. Listing one of these codes does not establish that every seafarer must hold a standalone endorsement with the source's stated name. Rank, assigned duties, ship type and administration requirements require separate verification. |
| Grain and IMSBC | The source groups grain/mineral shifting under IMSBC. The [IMO IMSBC explanation](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/safety/pages/cargoesinbulk-default.aspx) explicitly excludes grain in bulk; grain carriage must be distinguished from other solid bulk cargo and checked against the International Grain Code. The translated row is retained for traceability, not as an assertion that IMSBC covers grain. |
| Tanker and IMDG wording | The generic “Dangerous Cargo Handling” and “packaged dangerous cargo” certificate labels were not validated as universal certificate titles. The single tanker row does not establish equivalent qualifications for oil, chemical and liquefied-gas carriers or distinguish training levels and assigned duties. |
| Revalidation | The [IMO STCW overview](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/humanelement/pages/stcw-convention.aspx) describes five-year refresher requirements for specified safety competencies. This does not validate a blanket five-year expiry period or identical renewal route for every certificate, endorsement, medical document and seaman's book. Verify the requirements for each credential individually. |

The exact contents and evidentiary role of the seaman's book, medical assessment intervals, sea-service thresholds, refresher alternatives and rank-specific duties were not individually verified. The source's “overall responsibility” description of the master should not be used to infer an exhaustive legal allocation of liability.
